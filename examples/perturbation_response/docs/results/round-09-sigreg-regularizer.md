# Round 9 — the encoder's regularizer: does SIGReg give a better cell representation than VICReg?

*Part of [the results ledger](index.md), which holds the standing verdict, the scoreboard and the settings every round shares. Previous: [round 8](round-08-size-aware-endpoint.md) · Next: [round 10](round-10-control-reference.md).*

**What changed.** Only the anti-collapse regularizer of Stage A. Every encoder so far used VICReg, which constrains second moments only: the spread of each embedding dimension and the covariance between dimensions. SIGReg, from LeJEPA (Balestriero and LeCun, 2025), asks the whole embedding distribution to look like an isotropic standard Gaussian. It projects the embeddings onto 1,024 random unit directions, redrawn every step, and penalizes each projection's departure from $\mathcal{N}(0, 1)$ with the Epps–Pulley statistic, a weighted distance between the empirical and the Gaussian characteristic functions. The predictor, the EMA teacher and the stop-gradient were kept, so any difference is the regularizer's. Three arms were trained at seeds 0, 1 and 2: VICReg retrained with its existing recipe, SIGReg on the context-token embeddings, and SIGReg on the cell vector, the mean of the context tokens. SIGReg used the paper's defaults untuned, weight $0.05$ in the convex form $0.95 \cdot \text{pred} + 0.05 \cdot \text{SIGReg}$. Nothing downstream was retrained: this round reads the encoder alone.

## Experimental settings

| arm | regularizer | applied to | weight | experiments, seeds 0, 1, 2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `vicreg` | VICReg | the context-token embeddings | $0.04$, as $\text{pred} + 0.04 \cdot \text{VICReg}$ | `norman_stage_a_vicreg_s{0,1,2}` |
| `sigreg_tok` | SIGReg | the context-token embeddings | $0.05$, convex form | `norman_stage_a_sigreg_tok_s{0,1,2}` |
| `sigreg_pool` | SIGReg | the cell vector, the mean of the context tokens | $0.05$, convex form | `norman_stage_a_sigreg_pool_s{0,1,2}` |

- **Stage A, identical across arms.** `01_pretrain_stage_a.py` on the training cells of the combo split, 96,322 cells, so no held-out combination is ever seen. Each cell's 5,000 genes are split into 50 tokens of 100 genes. Embedding width 256, encoder depth 6, predictor depth 2, 4 attention heads, 12 of the 50 tokens masked as targets, so 38 tokens form the context. 50 epochs, batch 256, learning rate $10^{-3}$. The arms differ only in the flags `--reg-kind`, `--reg-target` and `--reg-coef`.
- **SIGReg settings, the paper's defaults.** 1,024 random unit directions, drawn from their own random-number generator and redrawn every step. The Epps–Pulley statistic uses 17 trapezoid points on $[-5, 5]$ with a Gaussian weight and is averaged over directions. The statistic grows with the number of samples it is computed on, so on the token target it is computed separately for each of the 38 context positions, over that position's 256 cells, and averaged over positions, as LeJEPA averages over views. Computed over all $256 \times 38 = 9{,}728$ token rows at once it would be about 38 times stronger than on the cell vector at the same weight. Both SIGReg arms therefore test 256 samples at a time and differ only in what they regularize.
- **Why VICReg was retrained.** Only one VICReg encoder existed, at seed 42 (`norman_stage_a`). It is reported beside the arms, outside the contrasts.
- **Compute.** One A40 GPU, three trainings in parallel. An epoch took about 58 seconds for the VICReg and cell-vector arms and about 88 seconds for the token arm, and the whole round, from the drift gate to the comparison, took 3 hours 1 minute, run by [`run_sigreg_pod.sh`](../../run_sigreg_pod.sh).
- **Reading each encoder.** Two scripts, nothing trained downstream. `14_ceiling_analysis.py --arms identity linear` computes the linear readout: a closed-form ridge regression, penalty 1, from a cell's 256-number vector to its expression, fit on training cells only and applied to the real held-out cells of each pair. The predicted change for a pair is the mean of those predictions minus the mean control cell, which is what the readout's $\Delta r$ and size-aware error are computed from. `02_probe_cell_encoder.py` fits a logistic-regression probe for the perturbation's identity on the cells split, 8,000 training cells and 4,000 test cells, 237 perturbations, so chance is $0.004$. The same script reports the effective rank: the exponential of the entropy of the normalized singular values of the centered cell vectors. It is 256 when every direction carries equal spread and 1 when a single direction carries all of it.
- **Benchmark and statistics.** The twenty held-out combinations and each pair's top 20 genes, as in every round; joint paired bootstrap over pairs, 10,000 resamples, seed 0, max-$t$ simultaneous 95% intervals. See [what every round shares](index.md#what-every-round-shares).

**The pre-committed endpoint.** The ceiling analysis's linear readout: a closed-form ridge from the encoder's cell vector to expression, fit on training cells and applied to the real held-out cells of each pair, scored by $\Delta$-correlation on each pair's top 20 genes. It measures how much of the perturbation effect a linear map can read from the frozen representation, with no decoder involved. Two contrasts in one max-$t$ family, each SIGReg arm minus VICReg, seed-averaged per pair. An arm passes if its contrast is at least $+0.02$ with a simultaneous interval wholly above zero, and is worse if the interval is wholly below zero. A separate rule, fixed in the same protocol, decides whether to go on to retraining the decoder and flow on a SIGReg encoder: it fires if the arm's seed-mean probe accuracy or effective rank exceeds VICReg's three-seed maximum. Both gates held: every identity arm scored $1.000$, and the existing VICReg encoder re-scored its stored $0.85214$ exactly.

**Verdict: neither SIGReg arm passes.**

| arm | linear readout $\Delta r$ | contrast with VICReg | simultaneous 95% interval | pairs higher than VICReg | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| VICReg (retrained, 3 seeds) | $0.853$ | | | | |
| SIGReg on tokens | $0.747$ | $-0.106$ | $[-0.282, +0.070]$ | $0/20$ | does not pass |
| SIGReg on the cell vector | $0.756$ | $-0.097$ | $[-0.244, +0.050]$ | $1/20$ | does not pass |

The simultaneous intervals include zero, so by the rule neither arm is formally worse. Descriptively they are lower on essentially every pair, and both unadjusted intervals lie below zero. The width of the simultaneous intervals comes from one pair: on CEBPE+RUNX1T1 the readout falls from $0.92$ with VICReg to $0.10$ and $0.14$ with SIGReg, while the other nineteen differences lie between $-0.25$ and $0$. Bootstrap resamples that leave that pair out have very small standard errors, so the studentized statistic is heavy-tailed and the max-$t$ critical value is $4.63$, about twice what a two-contrast family usually gets. The verdict stands as the rule gives it.

**Secondary results, intervals only, no verdicts.**

- *Size-aware error of the readout*, round 8's $e_p$: VICReg $0.543$ $[0.479, 0.615]$, SIGReg on tokens $0.806$, on the cell vector $0.794$. Both SIGReg arms are higher by about $0.25$, with intervals wholly above zero. For comparison, the additive reference scores $0.289$ and the round-8 learned models $1.05$ to $1.26$.
- *Interaction recovery of the readout*: VICReg $0.34$, SIGReg $0.13$ in both arms, lower by about $0.21$ with intervals wholly below zero.
- *Perturbation-identity probe*: test accuracy about $0.10$ for every SIGReg encoder against $0.055$ to $0.061$ for VICReg, with chance at $0.004$.
- *Effective rank of the cell vectors*: $216$ to $232$ of $256$ for VICReg, $20$ to $22$ for SIGReg on tokens and $16$ to $21$ on the cell vector. No run crossed the collapse flag, an effective rank below $10$.

**The retraining rule fired, and the reason it fired is suspect.** Both SIGReg arms exceed VICReg's probe accuracy, so the frozen rule triggers retraining of the decoder and flow on a SIGReg encoder. But the probe's training accuracy tells a different story than its test accuracy. On VICReg encoders the logistic probe reaches $0.80$ to $0.86$ on the cells it was fit to and $0.06$ on new cells. On SIGReg encoders it reaches about $0.115$ on both. The VICReg probe is memorizing its 8,000 training cells in a 256-dimensional space, and the SIGReg probe cannot, because the representation has only about twenty dimensions of variance. The higher test accuracy may therefore reflect a probe that overfits less rather than a representation that carries more about the perturbation. The rule is reported as it fired.

**Decision.** Because the signal that fired the rule is more plausibly probe overfitting than extra information, and the readout is worse on every endpoint, the downstream retraining was not run. The regularizer swap is set aside, and the next round turns to the decoder through the [co-adaptation program](../../co-adaptation/README.md), where the ceiling located the largest loss. Full LeJEPA, which also removes the teacher, was conditional on a pass and is not run on this basis.

**Diagnosis, after the fact and exploratory.** Measured on 4,096 training cells for the seed-0 encoders, SIGReg's cell vectors put essentially all their variance in about twenty directions: the top twenty eigenvalues of the covariance hold more than $99\%$ of it, against $21\%$ for VICReg. This is not the final LayerNorm switching dimensions off, since most of its scale parameters stay well above zero. It is a property of the regularizer at this weight. Each random one-dimensional projection of a Gaussian that lives in a twenty-dimensional subspace, scaled so that its total variance matches $\mathcal{N}(0, I_{256})$, is still Gaussian, with a variance that differs from one only by chance across directions. So SIGReg penalizes this kind of dimensional collapse only mildly. Simulated at batch size 256, the statistic is $1.07$ for an isotropic Gaussian, $3.85$ at rank 20 and $6.86$ at rank 10. At weight $0.05$ that is a cost of about $0.14$, which the prediction loss can outweigh. VICReg's variance term instead requires every dimension to keep a standard deviation of at least one, and it keeps the readout's extra dimensions alive. The rank SIGReg leaves appears to be enough to tell perturbations apart and not enough to read out expression linearly. Whether a larger SIGReg weight, or more directions, would hold the rank up was not tested.

**What it means.** At the paper's default setting, swapping VICReg for SIGReg makes the frozen representation worse for the thing this pipeline needs from it: the readout of expression change, on correlation, on size and on interaction. The diagnosis above points to a likely cause, a regularizer that barely resists dimensional collapse at this weight, and SIGReg as configured is not a route to closing the readout gap.

**What it does not mean.** It does not test full LeJEPA, which also removes the EMA teacher and the stop-gradient, and it does not test SIGReg at any other weight. It reads the encoder alone; the full pipeline was not retrained. It is scoped to the twenty held-out pairs and their top-20 genes.

**Script and report.** [`run_sigreg_pod.sh`](../../run_sigreg_pod.sh) trains and reads the nine encoders; [`22_compare_encoders.py`](../../22_compare_encoders.py) holds the frozen protocol in its docstring and writes `output/sigreg_compare.json`. SIGReg lives in `ssllab.objectives.jepa_loss`.
